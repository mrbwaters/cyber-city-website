"Do you think we should build, like, a RoboCorp triumphal arch? Like the Romans? Or is that passé?"

Bennie was silent. Her arms were folded across her chest, eyes shut tight, face set like stone. Chadwick Biggins was… He was a lot. She knew she'd given him her word that she would help, that she had a responsibility to him and to herself.

But by Henry, was he a lot.

"That's not quite it, Chad," Animar said softly. She had dialed into Chad's handheld and her voice was tinny on the thing's cheap speakers.

Benny grunted. "One more time, from the top."

"Humor me. Please."

Once Hyrak had done a proper deep scan of him and confirmed there was nothing in him or on him that could report his movements, Mortimer had donated some old clothing for him to wear. Eight-Ball.

Bennie could feel Chad watching her as she set up the PlayLand console she used for work. She heard him sniff dismissively as she manually configured it, running cables from the bulky unit to the input/output ports on her body.

"You don't have wireless?"

"Don't trust it. Just one more chip in your body that can get hacked."

Chad grunted. She knew from Hyrak's scans of the man that he clearly didn't agree with her. Setting aside the undeniable convenience of being able to mentally connect to PlayLand, she'd been in Cyber City long enough to know that there was a status that came with that convenience, too.

"What is that, a 2077 model?"

"Older," Bennie said simply.

"Wow." Chad shook his head. "You trust your brain with hardware that old?"

"Outside's old," Bennie said as she ran one last diagnostic. "Inside isn't."

"Oh. You upgraded it?"

"Yeah." "Yourself?"

Bennie gave Chad a smile and a nod. She had. She was proud of the work, too. She could love the hardware even if she didn't love what it did.

Bennie lay back on the breakroom couch and connected the last cable to the port behind her ear. The boot-up process began, the countdown to log-in overlaying itself over her vision. "God willing, this will be fast," she said to Chad, then she shut her eyes and tried to relax. The countdown ended, the Broma logo appeared, and Bennie willed it away before it could complete its silly little animation.

Bennie popped into existence in the generic PlayLand starting area, a simulacrum of a bedroom meant to serve as a customizable personal space. The lighting was dim but warm. Outside of the fixed window, the sky was a perpetual twilight. The bed was a twin with a single pillow, beige sheets, and a grey comforter. The one item she'd bothered to upgrade was the desk, and various notes and designs lay strewn across the top.

Before Bennie could interact with anything in the room, a pop-up filled her vision. "Animar has sent you a friend invite! Accept?"

"Okay," she muttered to herself. "Here we go." Bennie accepted the friendship invite. Another pop-up followed immediately: "Warp to Animar's location?" Bennie accepted that, too, and the world went dark. The Broma logo jerkily moved through its start-up animation. The world returned and Bennie was standing somewhere on PlayLand's version of Libbey Street. She felt a brief moment of nausea as the detail and texture and sensation returned to the world, everything around her resolving from monocolored blocks into proper buildings.

"Ms. Shrapnel?" came a voice from behind her. "Are you Bennie Shrapnel?"

Bennie turned around, and she felt her breath catch in her throat in a way that sent a flush of embarrassment to her face. The woman before her was gorgeous. She had fair skin, dark hair, dark clothes. Her almond-shaped eyes glowed like some ancient industrial CRT monitor, and she wore a lipstick so dark green that it appeared almost black. Fiber optic cables were threaded seamlessly into her hair, or maybe grew from her scalp, and they shifted slowly through all the colors of the rainbow.

This is what I always imagined the people in Cyber City would look like when I was a kid.

The woman stuck out her hand. "Animar. It's a pleasure to meet you, Ms. Shrapnel."

Bennie glanced down at the offered hand. Her veins pulsed with color like pure energy flowed through them. A lot of time and attention had gone into this avatar. She took the hand and shook it. "Call me Bennie. Please."

Animar nodded and smiled. "Bennie."

Bennie looked Animar up and down one last time, then glanced around. "This is it?"

"This is it."

Bennie blinked and looked up at the building. It was five stories and decrepit, its appearance probably close to how it actually looked in the real world. The first floor wall was painted black. The next two stories had windows, but the final two were just bare concrete. "This is what a hacker-owned nightclub looks like?" Bennie asked.

Animar nodded, a confident smile on her face. "I cross-referenced the physical deed and the digital one. They're a mess of shell companies, so someone's clearly made a serious effort to hide who owns what, but they couldn't help themself from making a few octopus puns. I'm very confident Tako-san owns this spot."

"Huh," Bennie said. "Nice work. So how do we get in? This is supposed to be an exclusive spot, right?"

Animar's smile grew into a proper grin. "I hacked the VIP list and got us on it. Shouldn't be a problem."

Bennie arched an eyebrow. Okay, then, she thought. Girl's got some skills. "Nicely done," she said.

"Thank you." Bennie turned to enter and Animar put a hand on her shoulder. "Oh, one moment."

"Hm?"

Animar looked Bennie in the eyes for just a moment, then glanced away. "I… Well. If we're supposed to be VIPs, I think we should look the part, don't you?"

Bennie stared at Animar in confusion for a moment then glanced down at herself. She was in a default PlayLand skin, blue pants and a white shirt. She never bothered to change the outfit, even when she was meeting with a client digitally.

"Shit. Um. I guess I can buy a dress or something," she mumbled, mentally opening the PlayLand storefront.

"Oh, please, let me," Animar said. Before Bennie could reply, Animar took a step back and walked in a circle around Bennie. Bennie swallowed and looked around, a touch uncomfortable under Animar's intense gaze.

What, she's going to buy it for me? Bennie thought. Animar gave her a curt nod, then closed her eyes and began weaving her hands through the air. Oh, she's actually sculpting it, Bennie realized. Soon, another pop-up appeared in Bennie's vision. "Animar has sent you a gift! Accept?"

A spinning 3D model of Bennie appeared before her vision. Her generic clothing had been traded out for a skintight grey suit with I/O ports for powered components to attach to. A high-visibility yellow roll cage protected her chest, and exoskeletal braces ran down her arms and legs. A gauntlet of tools on her right forearm and a diagnostic console on her left said that she was both an essential component of something larger and formidable in her own right.

As a young girl, she would have thought this was the coolest damn thing she'd ever seen. And even now, she had to admit, it was pretty damn cool.

"What do you think?" Animar asked. "Do you like it?" There was something hopeful in her voice, an eagerness that made Bennie grin.

"Does it come in orange?"

"Animar and B_Shrapnel?" the bouncer asked, his voice a deep rumble.

The window shut. Something clicked, and a small door opened within the mural. "Come in, ladies," the bouncer said, stepping aside to welcome them into a darkened foyer. Now she could see his avatar featured an octopus atop an overly muscled, humanoid body. He held up his hand and snapped, and Bennie felt her muscles freeze. It was time for the terms of service.

There were plenty of unusual provisions in the ToS, but nothing really surprised Bennie. Collision detection was automatically enabled and could not be disabled. Digital intoxication was automatically enabled and could not be disabled. Consent contracts were automatically enabled and could not be disabled. Communication channels to outside spaces were limited to one-on-one audio and text only. Content creation is not allowed inside the club without express permission from the owner. By visiting the club, she agreed to the use of her voice and likeness in promotional material. Anyone who violated or attempted to violate the Terms of Service would be banned from the club for life.

Bennie quickly agreed to everything and glanced back at Animar. Animar gave her a nod then told the bouncer they were ready. Another door opened, and light and music poured through. "Please enjoy your time with us, ladies," he rumbled.

Animar turned to Bennie and grinned. "Welcome to the Octopus's Garden, Bennie."

Bennie had grown up hearing that the right places in PlayLand were just as good as actually being somewhere, and that had never been true in her experience. Maybe for people like Chad and Animar who had high-end consoles and ultra-definition implants, but Bennie had always experienced PlayLand as more interesting than amazing.

The Octopus's Garden actually was amazing.

The inside was impossible, literally impossible. The environment seemed to have layers of relative gravity, with the people on the ground moving normally, but people floating in the air above her, suspended as if treading water, moving over and around and under each other as if swimming. Japanese characters floated over the bar, タコの庭, and the drinks promised ego death, dissociation, synesthesia. A localized heads-up display offered eight channels of music to choose from, and the virtual haptics were on point, with the bass thumping deep in her chest. Every surface felt perfectly textured and mapped under both hand and foot, and the code for the club was so optimized that Bennie didn't notice a single frame of lag anywhere. Beside her, Animar spun in a small circle, taking it all in.

Bennie looked around, too. There were plenty of avatars just dressed in their favorite outfits, as she and Animar were, and plenty more that were on theme as ocean-inspired creatures or in islander-inspired apparel. There were avatars of people that were clearly just here to mingle and be seen: corpos, some small groups in fetish gear, a few in gang colors out of the Lows and Bayside. And high above them at the top of the club, Tako-san loomed over them all, their avatar a many-armed and many-eyed biomechanical giant that operated eight turntables simultaneously.

Bennie felt a chill crawl up her back. Something about all of this didn't sit right with her, and it took her a moment to figure out what; almost none of the groups were mingling with each other. The corpos kept to themselves, the gangers with the gangers, the fet-heads with the fet-heads. Everyone seemed to be trying to stake out a corner of this incredible space for themselves, rather than enjoying each other's company and the shared identity that came from just being together and having a good time.

Bennie thought of the dances from her childhood, the fun that she and the other girls and boys had together before they got older and started to really notice each other. Before their parents all began to take an active interest in matching them up and pairing them off. There'd always been a sense of community there, of generations getting together and celebrating the seasons, their faith, each other.

God, I miss the dances now? Bennie winced. She hadn't thought about them in almost two decades, and here she was wondering if the Harvest Festival would be the way she remembered it.

Bennie shook the thought from her head and opened an encrypted channel to Animar. [Okay, we're in,] she sent. [What's the play?]

[I've secured a VIP meet and greet with Tako-san. Once this set's over, we'll be invited to their green room, and we can have a conversation in private.]

[How long until then?]

[Thirty minutes, I'd guess.]

Bennie nodded. Before she could reply, a notification popped up in the corner of her vision. Chad. Probably he was too impatient to wait the thirty minutes. She opened the message and Chad's voice filled her head.

"Dude, Bennie! This place looks fucking amazing! I'm, like, 90% sure I've been to the meatspace version of this club, but oh, my God, this is so much better!"

Bennie blinked in surprise, then annoyance. "Chad, get off the line!" she hissed. "We're only supposed to message each other if there's an emergency."

"Like, the swimming people!" Chad said, ignoring her completely. "They've got a tank with people dressed like mermaids, but… Is that guy in the pig mask naked? Bennie, turn a little to your left. I want to see if that guy's avatar is naked or if he's just pink."

"Absolutely not." Bennie turned back to Animar. "So," she said aloud, the encrypted channel forgotten. "What do we do until the meet and greet? Keep a low profile?"

Animar shrugged. "Have a drink? Dance a little? I've always wondered what it would be like to have synesthesia."

"That feels… reckless. We're here for business."

"Counterpoint," Chad said. Bennie winced, realizing he could hear their conversation on the monitor. "She should totally do it! 'Keeping a low profile' is what losers do. Try the weird drinks! Do it in my memory!"

"We can't do it in your memory, you're not dead," Bennie groused.

"My honor, then. Do it in my honor. I want to see if being able to see colors and shit changes the visuals on the monitor."

"Why would it?"

"I don't know. Because PlayLand! I don't know how it works."

Bennie sighed and turned to Animar but she was gone. She had already made her way to the bar, the crowd alternately parting before her and tripping over itself to buy her drinks.

Forty-five minutes passed and Tako-san finally wrapped up her set. Bennie sat at the bar and watched people come and go, but Animar had moved from one group to the other, dancing and laughing and being carefree in a way that made Bennie feel a pang of jealousy.

One of the club's staff, an octopode monstrosity nearly identical to the one manning the door, guided them behind the bar and down a hallway that ended in a single unmarked door. The monster knocked and a cheerful voice called out, "Come in!" He gave them a nod, opened the door, and stepped aside. Bennie muttered under her breath, "Whoa," and Animar cooed in delight.

Tako-san's room looked like an underwater cavern, an arena of dark stone walls with incongruously fancy furniture. Shelving had been carved into the walls, and Tako-san's seeming treasures were displayed there: photos of celebrities, antique computers and electronics, bits of code from programs preserved and displayed mid-execution. "This is beautiful," Animar murmured.

Laughter, high and amused, came down from above. "Thank you! It's kind of a perpetual work in progress, but I'm rather happy with it at the moment." Bennie and Animar looked up to see Tako-san floating above them. Instead of the biomechanical thing that had been DJing out in the Garden, they now took a more humanoid form, albeit nude and sexless and accented with fins and tentacles.

"Thank you both so much for joining me backstage!" Tako-san said with a warm smile. "It's just so energizing to connect with my fans. Honestly, it's my favorite thing about making music."

Bennie glanced over at Animar. Animar stood enthralled, studying the room intensely, and Bennie sent her a private message. [Lock down the room. Time to talk.]

Half a dozen expressions played across Tako-san's face in seconds. Confusion. Fear. Fury. An eerie calm. Tako-san flicked her wrist, and Bennie felt a wave of nausea roll over her as the world seemed to tremble around her before stabilizing. Tako-san looked surprised, but that soon gave way to irritation.

That's how you stay alive in this line of work." Tako-san sat, picked up a champagne flute, and took a sip. They turned towards Animar. "Remote access integration unit. If I can't leave, neither can you."

Fuck this. Chad was just a distraction. She muted him and tried to focus on what Animar and Tako-san were saying.

Chad Biggins, his PlayLand avatar probably styled exactly like how he saw himself every time he looked in a mirror.

"So, here. I want to offer you something more than money. I want to offer you a way out. I want to offer you a guarantee that you don't need to be afraid anymore."

Tako-san was silent. They were looking at Chad again, their face twisted in disbelief. But at least they're paying attention, Bennie thought. And she was, too, as much as she hated to admit it.

Chad saw that he had Tako-san's attention and smiled a little. "Let me ask you a question; have you ever been to the Schengen Empire?"

Tako-san blinked in confusion. "I… No?"

Chad went on. "My family has a château there. Beautiful place. Lovely weather. Hidden behind so many holding companies and subsidiaries of subsidiaries that I'm not even sure it technically legally exists." Chad smiled, his eyes suddenly warm again. "It's the kind of spot that's perfect to get away from it all, you know? Lay low for a while." He shrugged, playing up the guilelessness of it all. "I'm sending you the access codes right now. You just give me a name and a motive, and we all walk away. Pretty good deal, right?" Chad ran his hand through his hair as if he was wiping away sweat.

Tako-san was silent. Bennie could see that they were biting their cheek. They're thinking about it. Holy hell, they're actually thinking about it.

But they don't look like they're going to bite.

Chad must have seen it, too, because he took a deep breath and let it out slowly. "Hey," he said. "Can I tell you about my dad?" Without waiting for a response, he raised his hands and made a frame with his fingers. "This is Thaddeus Gentian Biggins." Thaddeus's official portrait appeared before him, drawn from his own memories.

By Henry, he looks just like Chad.

"He was a good guy. He wasn't perfect, not as a businessman or a father. But he tried to be good. More than that, he looked at the CEOs of the other zaibatsus in Cyber City, and he tried to be better." There was a strained quality to his voice, as if he was fighting to get the words out. He doesn't look well, Bennie thought. The sweat was visible on his avatar now, his skin turning wan.

Chad pinched the side of his temple, retrieving another memory, a more detailed one. "Here he is getting his face smashed in." Chad threw the memory into the room and everyone gasped. The footage was bloody and brutal, captured from a camera that must have been mounted on the wall directly above the murder. The EV-209's hydraulic breaker reduced Thaddeus to little more than a stain on the carpet.

Tako-san let out a strangled noise, shocked and unable to look away.

Bennie turned from the brutal image to Chad. He was facing the wall, digital tears welling up in his avatar's eyes. Bennie felt her heart break a little for him.

"Tako-san," he said, his voice choking. "I understand why you're afraid. Whoever did this is a monster. Help me get him."

Tako-san shook their head. "I'm not going to get myself killed for your revenge. I'm sorry."

Chad stepped toward Tako-san, more upset than Bennie had ever seen him, more upset even than the night he'd shown up on her doorstep. "I don't want revenge," he shouted. "I want… I want this to make sense. My dad was a good man." His voice was cracking now. "He was a good man! And someone did this to him! And you know who and you won't tell me?"

"He'll kill me!" Tako-san cried out. "You'll try and get him, and he'll kill you, and he'll come for me!"

"No, he won't, because you'll be in the Schengen Empire!"

"I'll be trapped! I don't know anyone there! I won't have friends, or resources, or anything!"

Somewhere behind her, Bennie heard Animar say, "Chad? I'm picking up some weird traffic around you. I think you should log off."

But his eyes were on Bennie. She'd just blurted out, "Then we'll sweeten the deal. Eight million bits. But only if you talk right now."

Tako-san's jaw dropped. Chad arched an eyebrow and coughed. Animar sputtered.

"Eight million? What is that, your take for the RoboCorp job?" Tako-san asked.

Bennie inhaled, exhaled. It's money I should have never had. "It's eight million bits. You really care where it comes from?"

Tako-san stared at her, their face uncertain for a moment before settling into a mask of business-like impassiveness. "Deal. Send it," they said.

"Half now, half after you talk."

Tako-san nodded.

Bennie mentally opened her account and created a transfer of four million bits. Her bank did everything it could to stop her, a pop-up asking her, "Are you sure?"

Was she? That was four million bits worth of new materials, rent, payroll. Steak dinners.

She closed her eyes and authorized the transfer to Tako-san. She heard them let out a low whistle. "Holy shit. Okay." They cleared their throat. "It was Nikolai Kuragin. Derrig's a Kulak, been working for him for years. I overheard them talking and Kuragin said Thaddeus Biggins was moving in on his territory and he needed to go."

Chad's jaw dropped. "Nikolai fucking Kuragin had my dad killed? What the hell for? My dad was never involved in organized crime!"

"I don't know anything about that," Tako said, their voice flat. "All I know is what I heard, alright?"

"Chad," Animar pleaded, "you need to log off, and the room is locked down. I can't force you out."

The sweat was pouring off Chad now, and Animar looked like she was on the verge of a panic attack.

"Chad, someone knows you're here! You need to log off before–"

Concern began to flood Tako-san's face, and their eyes glazed over as they began monitoring the code flowing in and out of the room. "What are you…" they began to say, and then their voice turned to a shriek. "That's a spike! That's a fucking spike! Get out of here, now!"

Chad began coughing hard. Bennie moved over to him and put her hand on his back. "Chad? Are you okay?"

He looked up at her and she gasped. Blood trickled out of his nose and his mouth, and his hand was full of thick clots of it. He fell to the ground twitching.
